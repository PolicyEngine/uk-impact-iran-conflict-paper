\begin{abstract}
\noindent I trace the 2026 Iran conflict energy shock from wholesale markets to individual UK households. Macroeconomic scenarios for oil and gas are mapped into an Ofgem price-cap path and a pump-price path, with gas and electricity shocked asymmetrically because gas sets the British electricity price roughly 85 per cent of the time, and the resulting price vector is passed through PolicyEngine UK's Family Resources Survey microdata. Under the central scenario the mean household loses \pounds \genCentralLossMean{} a year, equal to \genCentralLossPctIncome{} per cent of equivalised disposable income, giving an aggregate of \pounds \genAggSpendRealisedBn{} billion against the Resolution Foundation's independently published \pounds 11 billion of extra household energy and fuel spend at sustained peaks; an upper-bound specification that applies observed peak pump prices undamped for a full year gives \pounds \genPeakFuelAggBn{} billion, so the two specifications bracket the only comparable published estimate. The incidence is regressive in per cent and non-monotonic in pounds: the bottom decile loses \genDecileOneLossPct{} per cent of income against \genDecileTenLossPct{} per cent for the top, while in cash the largest loss falls on decile eight (\pounds \genDecileEightLossGbp{}), not decile ten (\pounds \genDecileTenLossGbp{}). The reason for the cash profile is the paper's most consequential finding: motor fuel carries between \genMotorFuelShareOfLoss{} and \genPeakFuelMotorFuelShareOfLoss{} per cent of the aggregate loss, against \genGasShareOfLoss{} per cent through gas and \genElecShareOfLoss{} per cent through electricity in the main specification. Every domestic-bill instrument in the current policy debate therefore addresses at most about half of the loss by construction, which---rather than targeting design---is the deepest reason all five proposals I score underperform: a means-tested social tariff and the universal discounted block both leave \genSocialTariffUncompensatedShare{} per cent of losers uncompensated, and only the flat rebate compensates a majority. Within-decile dispersion of the burden exceeds the between-decile gradient. The steepness of the percentage gradient is uncertain by roughly a factor of two: recalibrating motor-fuel spend to ONS Family Spending, which the microdata over-imputes at the bottom of the distribution, takes the decile-one to decile-ten ratio from \genDecileRatioPct{} to \genOnsFuelDecileRatioPct{} to one, though the burden falls in income under both. The analysis is static and use-side only, and is therefore a lower bound on total incidence.
\end{abstract}
